# Meet the 51 startups chasing 2026's SA crown

There are 51 different startups on the 2026 South African Startup Awards shortlist, spread across 11 sector categories plus People's Choice, and most of them are fixing very ordinary South African headaches. Think slow crime reporting, pricey weight-loss drugs, domestic workers with no payslips, couriers with nowhere to sit, and school-list chaos every January. The winners get announced on the evening of **18 November 2026** at a ceremony in Cape Town (also livestreamed), after public voting ran from 2 to 6 November ([Bizcommunity](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/entries-open-for-2026-south-african-startup-awards-747882a)). When this was written, the winners hadn't been published yet. Startup Club ZA also took down its own short descriptions of each finalist once voting closed, so every profile below is pieced together from the startups' own websites, press coverage and LinkedIn posts. The finalist names and categories come straight from the organiser's published data, so those are solid. What you're getting for each one is the website (where there is one), a line or two on what they offer, and a line on the problem they solve. Where I'm less than sure the company I found is the right one, I've said so in a short note.

## AI & Big Data: two marketing brains, a safety network, a cyber shop and an AI builder

### Brand Scan
**Website:** [brandscan.co.za](https://brandscan.co.za/)

**What they offer:** A Cape Town company, registered in 2025, that checks how often AI assistants like ChatGPT recommend your brand, then gives you a step-by-step plan to show up more. This is called GEO, short for "generative engine optimisation", which is basically SEO for AI chatbots instead of Google ([Brand Scan](https://brandscan.co.za/)).

**Problem they solve:** More shoppers now ask an AI what to buy, and big brands (it works mostly with FMCG and insurance companies) have no idea whether they're being recommended or quietly left out.

### Community Wolf
**Website:** communitywolf.com

**What they offer:** A public-safety platform where residents report crime and suspicious activity through WhatsApp, with no app to download, and the reports are pooled into community intelligence for security responders ([ITWeb](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/whatsapp-based-public-safety-platform-gets-r83m/KA3Ww7dznBYqrydZ)). In December 2025 it bought Namola, the emergency app that connects people to police, medics and private security ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/?p=175575)).

**Problem they solve:** Crime reporting and emergency response in South Africa are slow and scattered across too many groups and channels.

### KRITIQ
**Website:** [kritiq.app](https://kritiq.app/)

**What they offer:** A Johannesburg-built marketing dashboard that pulls your ads, website traffic, content, SEO and AI-search visibility into one place, tells you what to fix first, and drafts the fix for you. Plans start at R2,499 a month, and a sister services arm, KRITIQ Studio, does the hands-on work ([KRITIQ](https://kritiq.app/); [KRITIQ Studio](https://kritiqstudio.com/)).

**Problem they solve:** Marketers are drowning in separate tools and reports and can't easily see which problem is actually costing them.

### Kwanda AI
**Website:** [kwanda.ai](https://kwanda.ai/)

**What they offer:** A Stellenbosch and Cape Town "AI enablement partner" that designs, builds and maintains custom AI agents and automations for businesses, plugged into the company's own systems. It's the first spin-out of Adii Pienaar's AI venture studio Ubundi, and it was nominated alongside its new product, Ayda ([The Open Letter](https://theopenletter.io/p/kwanda-ai-enablement)).

**Problem they solve:** Most businesses know AI could save them time but don't have the people to work out where it fits or to build and run it.

### Subverted
**Website:** subverted.io

**What they offer:** A cybersecurity firm that does penetration testing (hiring friendly hackers to break into your systems before real ones do), runs a gamified cyber-skills academy, and launched "Proactive", a single platform for running all of that testing continuously ([ITWeb](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/south-african-start-up-subverted-challenges-norms-of-proactive-security-at-itweb-security-summit-2026/rxP3jqBEbWgMA2ye)).

**Problem they solve:** Companies test their security with a patchwork of tools and providers, which creates noise and leaves gaps where real risk slips through.

## Deeptech: lab science and hardware with real-world stakes

### C Star
**Website:** not confirmed

**What they offer:** A University of Cape Town spin-off, founded in 2022, developing technology that turns captured CO2 into liquid fuels that can be made in smaller, local plants. It was the first Africa-based team picked for the Breakthrough Energy Fellows programme ([Engineering News](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/uct-spin-off-c-star-selected-for-breakthrough-energy-fellows-programme-2025-09-19)).

**Problem they solve:** Liquid fuels are hard to make climate-friendly, and C Star recycles CO2 into fuel instead of pumping more fossil carbon out of the ground.

### Nanosene
**Website:** not confirmed

**What they offer:** A Stellenbosch University spin-out that makes special polymers (branded BzAM) which gently lift proteins out of cell membranes without the harsh chemicals labs usually use. It sells to drug companies and research labs, and has a manufacturing deal with Germany's Cube Biotech ([SU Innovus](https://www.su.ac.za/en/innovus/technology-transfer/nanosene-pty-ltd)).

**Problem they solve:** Membrane proteins are some of the most important drug targets, but they're very hard to isolate intact, which slows down drug discovery ([KRISP](https://krisp.org.za/news.php?id=966)).

### Safeguard Technologies
**Website:** not found

**What they offer:** A student-led Stellenbosch startup building a layered personal-safety system: a mobile app, a wearable panic button that works without a phone or signal, and drones that respond to alerts. It came second in the 2025 RISP Regional Innovation Challenge ([Die Matie](https://diematie.com/2025/09/student-led-startup-secures-funding-for-crime-fighting-drones/)).

**Problem they solve:** When you're in danger and have no phone or network coverage, getting help fast is close to impossible.

*Confidence: medium. Only one source (the university student paper) covers this company, so the match rests on the name and the deeptech fit.*

### Tavas
**Website:** [tavas.tech](https://www.tavas.tech/)

**What they offer:** A Cape Town company founded in 2024 by ex-Tesla energy engineer Dirk Pauw. It sells a small AI computer that sits on site, plus a cloud platform, that automatically runs solar, battery and mini-grid systems, keeps the important stuff powered during outages, and flags maintenance before things break ([Tavas](https://www.tavas.tech/); [We Are Tech Africa](https://www.wearetech.africa/fr/fils/tech-stars/dirk-pauw-deploie-une-ia-qui-optimise-lenergie-en-temps-reel)).

**Problem they solve:** Solar and battery setups are fiddly to configure and expensive to run well, and with an unreliable grid, a badly managed system means lights out.

## Climate & Sustainability: conservation, energy data and worms

### MeYouUs
**Website:** [meyouus.org](https://meyouus.org/)

**What they offer:** A donation platform where you pick a spot on a map of protected land or ocean, get a precise three-word address for your patch (using what3words), and your money goes to the conservation group that looks after it. You can grow your sponsored area outward from there ([MeYouUs](https://meyouus.org/)).

**Problem they solve:** Lots of people care about nature but have no easy, tangible way to support a specific place they love.

### Switch Energy (Pty) Ltd
**Website:** [switch.org.za](https://www.switch.org.za/)

**What they offer:** Smart electricity and water meters plus software that turns meter readings, tariffs and utility bills into accurate numbers for billing. It also handles "wheeling", which is when a private power producer sends electricity over Eskom or municipal lines to a buyer somewhere else, and someone has to track and settle who gets paid what ([Switch](https://www.switch.org.za/)).

**Problem they solve:** Property owners, municipalities and power traders work with messy, unreliable utility data, which makes billing and trading electricity a headache, especially as tariffs keep climbing ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/03/13/rising-electricity-costs-south-africa-energy-startups/)).

### WiggleWoo Worm Farm
**Website:** [wigglewoo.co.za](https://wigglewoo.co.za/)

**What they offer:** A weekly food-scrap collection service in Cape Town's Deep South (Kommetjie to Muizenberg). You fill a 10-litre bucket, it gets swapped each week, worms turn the scraps into rich compost, and you get 2 litres of it back every month ([WiggleWoo](https://wigglewoo.co.za/)).

**Problem they solve:** Food waste rotting in landfill gives off methane, a powerful greenhouse gas, and most households have no simple way to compost.

## Consumertech: cheaper meals, smart rings and tyres at your door

### Bites-pricecompare
**Website:** not found (app)

**What they offer:** A free app for comparing restaurant menu prices, photos and reviews across Cape Town. Its 20-year-old UCT founder, Dylan Steiner, personally visited about 700 restaurants to collect the menus ([TimesLIVE](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2026-10-21-r130-or-r79-uct-students-app-helps-diners-find-the-cheaper-bite/)).

**Problem they solve:** You can't easily tell if a meal is good value. Steiner found the same dish for R130 at one spot and R79 across the street.

### Doisk
**Website:** [doisk.com](https://doisk.com/)

**What they offer:** A small Cape Town AI and app studio. Its consumer apps are Enfant, which listens to a baby's cry and suggests what the baby might need, and Stylit, a beauty and wellness booking app that pays practitioners within about a day ([Doisk](https://doisk.com/)).

**Problem they solve:** Tired parents struggle to work out why their baby is crying, and beauty practitioners struggle with bookings and slow payouts.

### Driveway Tyres
**Website:** [drivewaytyres.co.za](https://drivewaytyres.co.za/)

**What they offer:** Buy tyres online, pick a time and place, and a mobile team comes to your home or office to fit and balance them. Fitting covers Pretoria, Centurion, Midrand and Joburg, and they'll deliver tyres anywhere in the country ([Driveway Tyres](https://drivewaytyres.co.za/info/)).

**Problem they solve:** Nobody wants to lose half a Saturday queuing at a tyre shop.

### VezoPay
**Website:** not confirmed

**What they offer:** A smart ring you tap to pay at any card machine. It never needs charging and links to your bank card through an app. It's live with Investec, Absa, FNB and RMB Private Bank, with rings from about R2,999 ([WeeTracker](https://weetracker.com/2026/08/14/vezopay-payment-ring-bank-bottleneck/)).

**Problem they solve:** You can pay without pulling out a wallet or phone, and because the ring stores a one-time code instead of your card details, losing it doesn't expose your bank info ([TechCentral](https://techcentral.co.za/vezopay-payment-ring-built-south-africans/247144/)).

### VISTO
**Website:** [visto.host](https://visto.host/us)

**What they offer:** A smart-TV app for Airbnb, guesthouse and hotel hosts that turns the guest TV into a branded welcome screen with Wi-Fi details (via QR code), house info and local restaurant tips in 20+ languages. There's a free plan and an ad-free paid one ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.digidash.themillennialcompany&hl=en)).

**Problem they solve:** Hosts answer the same guest questions ("What's the Wi-Fi password?") over and over.

*Confidence: medium. The developer is South African and the app is new (April 2026), but "Visto" is a common name and no source links this app to the awards.*

## Digital Commerce: boats, festivals, pop-ups, thrift and school kits

### Berth
**Website:** [berthapp.com](https://www.berthapp.com/)

**What they offer:** Software for marinas to run bookings, assign boat spaces and message boat owners in one system, plus a booking side where boaters can find and book a berth at places like Durban, Hout Bay and Simon's Town. It won its category at the EU's BlueInvest Africa 2026 ([EU Oceans & Fisheries](https://oceans-and-fisheries.ec.europa.eu/news/blueinvest-africa-2026-where-innovation-meets-opportunities-2026-11-05_en)).

**Problem they solve:** Marinas juggle bookings and communication across spreadsheets, calls and separate tools.

### SERVA
**Website:** not found

**What they offer:** An app for ordering food and drinks at concerts, festivals and sports events. You order on your phone, get updates, then collect or have it brought to you, while vendors and organisers get live sales data. It launched in December 2025 and passed R500,000 in sales within about two months ([HYPE Magazine](https://hypemagazine.co.za/2026/03/18/jr-bogopa-leads-the-launch-of-serva-a-category-defining-platform-built-for-the-future-of-events/)).

**Problem they solve:** Long queues at events frustrate attendees and cost vendors sales.

### SVYP
**Website:** [svyp.io](https://www.svyp.io/)

**What they offer:** A replacement for the "sign up for 10% off" pop-up on online shops. Shoppers swipe through a few quick picture questions to get their reward, and every answer goes into the brand's customer system as a preference. It works with Shopify ([SVYP](https://www.svyp.io/)).

**Problem they solve:** Normal pop-ups grab an email address but tell the brand nothing about what that shopper actually likes.

### Thrif
**Website:** not found

**What they offer:** A Cape Town marketplace, founded in 2024, for buying and selling new, pre-loved and vintage clothes. AI helps sellers write listings and tidy up photos, and sign-in and alerts run over WhatsApp ([Crunchbase](https://crunchbase.com/organization/thrif)).

**Problem they solve:** Good clothes are expensive, and perfectly wearable ones end up unused or thrown away. Thrif's quality-checked second-hand pieces sell at 50 to 70% off ([The Open Letter](https://theopenletter.beehiiv.com/p/fashion-tech-sa)).

### Zumii
**Website:** not found

**What they offer:** Parents WhatsApp their school stationery list, and Zumii buys everything, labels it and delivers a ready kit to the door. It also supplies companies and schools, and lets donors sponsor kits for kids who can't afford them ([We Are Tech Africa](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/tech-stars/south-african-entrepreneur-moloko-manthata-simplifies-school-supply-shopping-with-zumii)).

**Problem they solve:** Back-to-school shopping eats hours of a parent's time, and plenty of learners start the year without supplies.

## Edtech: from varsity study tools to AI skills for office teams

### Bag Learning (Pty) Ltd
**Website:** [baglearning.com](https://www.baglearning.com/)

**What they offer:** An AI study app for university students. You upload your own course notes and it turns them into summaries, flashcards, quizzes and mock exams, with an AI tutor called Zazi that answers questions from your material, in the app or on WhatsApp ([Bag Learning](https://www.baglearning.com/)).

**Problem they solve:** Most students can't afford a personal tutor, and bursary funders want a way to support the students they pay for.

### Coachbit
**Website:** coachbit.com

**What they offer:** An app that pairs a learner aged 11 to 21 with a real human coach who checks in daily and builds study habits, routines and organisation skills, rather than teaching a subject ([Welcome to the Jungle](https://app.welcometothejungle.com/companies/Coachbit)). It was started by Nick Miller, founder of tutoring company Teach Me 2.

**Problem they solve:** Lots of capable kids struggle not because the work is too hard, but because they can't plan, focus or keep themselves on track.

### Edufearn Pty (Ltd) / ta Edufern
**Website:** edufearn.co.za

**What they offer:** Mother-tongue and bilingual learning materials that match the national school curriculum (CAPS): subject dictionaries, workbooks, interactive storybooks that come alive through a phone camera, and educational games, mainly for under-resourced schools. It also trains teachers ([NASCEE](https://nascee.org.za/membership/members/the-edufearn-foundation)).

**Problem they solve:** Many children learn in a language that isn't their home language, and poorer schools lack materials that bridge that gap.

*Confidence: medium. The best source describes the Edufearn Foundation, a non-profit; the finalist is probably its commercial sister company.*

### Fluent
**Website:** [getfluent.co.za](https://getfluent.co.za/)

**What they offer:** AI training for workplace teams, started in Cape Town in April 2026. It begins with a free audit of how well your team uses AI, then runs a six-session programme on the team's real daily work, plus sessions for executives ([Fluent](https://getfluent.co.za/); [JT Cronje](https://www.jtcronje.co.za/)).

**Problem they solve:** Companies have bought AI tools, but most staff don't know how to use them well on their own work.

### Young Aspiring Thinkers
**Website:** not confirmed

**What they offer:** An education initiative founded by Thulani Masebenza that supports learners from disadvantaged schools with in-person and online learning, and connects young people to academic and career opportunities ([Podcast, Audible](https://audible.com.au/podcast/Episode-46-Thulani-Masebenza-Young-Aspiring-Thinkers/B09KH69LY1); [iNEEW](https://ineew.com/en/blog/salud-mental-77/lebo-233)).

**Problem they solve:** Kids at under-resourced schools miss out on the extra support and opportunities better-off learners take for granted.

*Confidence: medium-low. Very little is published about it, it was described as an NGO in 2021, and its current product and website couldn't be confirmed.*

## Fintech: farmers, investors, bonds, business cards and student funding

### eSusFarm
**Website:** not confirmed

**What they offer:** A platform from Midrand that gives small-scale farmers access to credit, insurance and weather-risk information. Farmers can sign up on a basic phone using USSD (those menus you get by dialling a code like \*120\#), and its insurance pays out automatically to mobile money when the weather crosses a set level, such as too little rain ([Microsoft Source](https://news.microsoft.com/source/emea/features/ai-smallholder-farmer-finance-esusfarm/)).

**Problem they solve:** Banks and insurers don't have the data to judge small farmers' risk, so those farmers get treated as "unbankable" and are wiped out by bad seasons.

### Fynbos Money
**Website:** not confirmed

**What they offer:** An investing app with a free emergency savings account and a free tax-free savings account (a special account where your growth isn't taxed, up to a yearly limit), plus a paid tier for families and more funds. It charges a flat subscription instead of a percentage of your money ([Disrupt Africa](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://disruptafrica.com/2025/02/11/how-sas-fynbos-money-is-helping-users-to-make-long-term-investments)).

**Problem they solve:** Percentage-based fees quietly eat your returns over the years, and DIY investing platforms are confusing.

### Intengo Market
**Website:** not confirmed

**What they offer:** An online marketplace where companies issue bonds (basically IOUs to investors) through online auctions, and big investors like asset managers bid for them. It was incubated by Rand Merchant Bank, and Aspen Pharmacare raised R410 million in its first auction ([IOL](https://www.iol.co.za/personal-finance/investments/rmb-launches-intengo-platform-for-corporate-debt-instruments-e0b13617-9269-4bc6-8891-b26fa8f4fee4)).

**Problem they solve:** Corporate borrowing in South Africa has mostly happened over phone calls and emails, which is slow and hard to see into ([BusinessLIVE](https://www.businesslive.co.za/bd/companies/financial-services/2022-08-24-intengo-market-looks-to-revolutionise-africas-corporate-debt-market/)).

### Paystashio
**Website:** [paystashio.com](https://www.paystashio.com/about-us)

**What they offer:** Company cards (physical and virtual) for small businesses, with spending limits per employee, real-time alerts, receipt snapping, automatic categorising and syncing to Xero and Sage. It was built by Byte Orbit, a South African firm that's made banking software for almost two decades, and launched in November 2026 ([The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/payments/news/paymentology-powers-byte-orbits-paystashio-for-south-african-smes)).

**Problem they solve:** Small businesses lose track of staff spending through shared cards, lost slips and messy expense claims.

### WaFunda
**Website:** not confirmed

**What they offer:** A Cape Town social enterprise that helps young people fund their studies and training, and runs education, work and entrepreneurship programmes. It brought UK student money-skills platform Blackbullion to South Africa in 2021 ([Blackbullion](https://business.blackbullion.com/press_release/wafunda-partnership-takes-blackbullion-into-south-africa/)).

**Problem they solve:** "Missing middle" students earn too much for government bursaries but too little to pay for university, so they get stuck.

## Future of Work: AI sidekicks for lawyers, accountants and service firms

### Anvaya Legal AI Assistant
**Website:** not confirmed

**What they offer:** An AI assistant for law firms and in-house legal teams that reviews contracts, summarises evidence, does research and drafts documents, all inside Microsoft Word and grounded in South African law. It won the SA leg of the 2026 Start-up World Cup ([ITWeb](https://www.itweb.co.za/article/sa-legal-ai-start-up-anvaya-wins-local-start-up-world-cup/GxwQDM1D3pn7lPVo)).

**Problem they solve:** Lawyers lose huge chunks of their day to admin spread across too many separate tools.

### Chronodesk
**Website:** [chronodesk.io](https://chronodesk.io/)

**What they offer:** Cloud software for service businesses (agencies, IT support firms, engineering consultancies) that combines projects, support tickets, client records, timesheets and billing in one place, with AI help built in. It's made by Johannesburg's Rysis Software ([Capterra AU](https://www.capterra.com.au/software/1321295/Chronodesk); [Serchen](https://www.serchen.com/company/chronodesk)).

**Problem they solve:** When projects, time tracking and invoicing live in different apps, billable hours slip through the cracks.

### Crystal AI
**Website:** not confirmed

**What they offer:** An AI recruitment tool from Cape Town's Job Crystal that screens and matches candidates for small businesses, designed to cut bias against women and people over 40. It expanded to the UK in 2024 ([TechCentral](https://techcentral.co.za/cape-town-start-up-launches-ai-powered-recruitment-platform/207266/); [Lionesses of Africa](https://www.lionessesofafrica.com/blog/2024/4/21/lioness-launch-job-crystal-launches-crystal-ai-in-england-spearheaded-by-ceo-sasha-knott)).

**Problem they solve:** Small businesses can't afford recruiters, hiring is slow and often biased, and job seekers rarely hear back.

*Confidence: medium. "Crystal AI" is a generic name, Job Crystal's tool is the only South African match, and there's little coverage of it after 2024.*

### Fintura
**Website:** not confirmed

**What they offer:** AI practice-management software for accounting firms that puts billing, tax, compliance, HR and audit work in one dashboard and links to the CIPC (the companies register) to automate filings. Founded in 2023, it won the SA Startup World Cup in 2025 ([Ventureburn](https://ventureburn.com/2025/08/fintura-heads-to-silicon-valley-after-winning-sa-startup-world-cup/)).

**Problem they solve:** Accountants waste hours hopping between disconnected systems and doing compliance paperwork by hand.

### LEVRA
**Website:** levra.me

**What they offer:** Workplace "people skills" training using virtual reality and AI role-play, so staff can practise tough conversations and get feedback, with a learning path tailored to each person. It won the Edtech category at the 2025 SA Startup Awards ([StartupMag](https://startupmag.co.za/?p=95581)).

**Problem they solve:** Employees often lack communication and resilience skills, and those are hard to build from slides and videos.

*Confidence: medium on the SA link. It's clearly the 2025 winner, but third-party profiles place it in London, founded in 2022 ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/levra)), and the South African co-founder link isn't verified.*

## Healthtech: AI scribes, mobile mammograms and cheaper weight-loss care

### GreenNotes
**Website:** [greennotes.co.za](https://greennotes.co.za)

**What they offer:** Digital patient-record software for clinicians, from George, with an AI scribe that listens to the consultation and writes the notes. It also handles scripts, referral letters, bookings, consent forms and medical-aid claims, from R960 per clinician a month ([GreenNotes](https://greennotes.co.za)).

**Problem they solve:** Doctors, physios and psychologists spend a big part of every day typing notes and chasing admin instead of seeing patients.

### Obiflex
**Website:** obiflex.co.za

**What they offer:** A doctor-led weight-loss programme run over WhatsApp, using GLP-1 medicines (the Ozempic-style drugs that curb appetite), paid off in instalments, for example 3 months for R1,499 over 5 payments ([LinkedIn, Riaz Motara](https://www.linkedin.com/in/riaz-motara-7ab47847/)).

**Problem they solve:** Registered GLP-1 treatments can cost R3,000 to R6,000 a month, which puts them out of reach for most people.

### Radhiant Diagnostic Imaging SA
**Website:** not confirmed

**What they offer:** Mobile clinics on trucks that bring 3D mammograms (breast cancer screening) and ultrasound to smaller Eastern Cape towns like Port Alfred, Makhanda and Butterworth. It's run by radiologist Dr Anith Chacko from East London and launched in 2023 ([press release](https://business.minstercommunitypost.com/minstercommunitypost/article/marketersmedia-2025-7-17-radhiant-diagnostic-imaging-sa-inc-introduces-south-africas-first-fully-mobile-mammography-service)).

**Problem they solve:** Women outside the big cities often have to travel far for a mammogram, so many just don't get screened.

### The Surgical Assistant (PTY) Ltd
**Website:** not confirmed

**What they offer:** An online marketplace that matches private surgeons who need an assistant in theatre with qualified doctors looking for paid work, often junior doctors fresh out of community service. It also sorts out their insurance and hospital registration, and takes a 15% fee per placement ([Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2024-12-17-surgical-assistant-is-just-what-sas-unemployed-doctors-ordered)).

**Problem they solve:** South Africa has unemployed young doctors on one side and surgeons struggling to find reliable assistants on the other.

### Traumsformed
**Website:** [traumsformed.com](https://www.traumsformed.com/)

**What they offer:** Self-guided trauma support led by an AI guide called "Robyn", who chats with you in your own words, works out where you are, and puts you on a 12-month programme, with referrals to a human therapist when needed. It costs R399 once for the year, with no auto-renewal ([Traumsformed](https://www.traumsformed.com/)).

**Problem they solve:** Trauma is common in South Africa, but therapy is expensive and many people don't recognise what they're carrying as trauma ([CSVR](https://help.csvr.org.za/?p=675)).

## Informal Market: domestic workers, field data and first jobs

### 2Klip
**Website:** [timbuk2.ai/2klip](https://www.timbuk2.ai/)

**What they offer:** A location game from Cape Town's Timbuk2 where locals earn rewards for answering real-world questions, like "Is this billboard up?" or "Is this product on the shelf at your spaza?" The answers become data that AI teams, retailers and advertisers can buy. It hit 50,000 sign-ups after a push from September ([TechFinancials](https://techfinancials.co.za/2026/11/11/ai-still-needs-people-on-the-ground-inside-south-africas-2klip/)).

**Problem they solve:** AI and brands have almost no fresh, on-the-ground information about townships and informal shops, and locals gain small ways to earn.

### AskMandla
**Website:** [askmandla.com](https://askmandla.com/about/)

**What they offer:** HR and payroll for households that employ a domestic worker, done entirely on WhatsApp with an AI helper called Mandla (and real people when needed). It handles contracts, UIF and COIDA registration (the government unemployment and injury-on-duty funds), payslips and leave ([AskMandla](https://askmandla.com/about/)).

**Problem they solve:** About 1.6 million domestic workers often go without contracts, payslips or unemployment cover, because employers find the paperwork confusing ([We Are Tech Africa](https://wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/solutions/askmandla-targets-1-6-million-domestic-workers-with-whatsapp-based-employment-platform)).

### FIRST JOBLY
**Website:** [firstjobly.co.za](https://firstjobly.co.za)

**What they offer:** A free job board for government jobs, learnerships, internships, graduate programmes and bursaries across all nine provinces. It comes with a CV maker, a filler for the Z83 (the form you need to apply for a government job), job alerts, an Android app and a WhatsApp channel ([FirstJobly](https://firstjobly.co.za)).

**Problem they solve:** Entry-level opportunities are scattered all over the place, and the application paperwork trips up first-time job seekers.

*Confidence: medium. The site is the only match for the name, but it shows no company details or founders.*

## Mobility & Logistics: fairer cabs, courier pods, bakkies and moving billboards

### Bro Cabs. Pty. Ltd
**Website:** [brocabs.co.za](https://www.brocabs.co.za/)

**What they offer:** A Johannesburg ride-hailing app, launched in February 2025, where drivers pay a flat subscription (about R150 a week) and keep 100% of their fares. ## Patterns: WhatsApp is the real operating system

A few things jump out when you see all 51 side by side. **WhatsApp is everywhere.** Community Wolf, Thrif, Zumii, Bag Learning's Zazi tutor, Obiflex, AskMandla, FIRST JOBLY, WiggleWoo's invoicing and LaaiMy's updates all run through it, and eSusFarm goes one step simpler with USSD for basic phones. These founders are building where people already are, not asking them to download yet another app. **AI is in nearly every category**, but it's mostly practical: writing notes, drafting legal documents, sorting expenses, tutoring from your own notes. Two of the AI & Big Data finalists, Brand Scan and KRITIQ, are even chasing the same brand-new problem of whether chatbots recommend your brand. And a lot of these are **access fixes**: bringing mammograms to small towns, payslips to domestic workers, insurance to small farmers and affordable meds to people priced out.

One thing to keep in mind if you're judging eligibility. The awards are meant for ventures **under five years old**, but several finalists look older on paper, going by public sources. These are The Surgical Assistant (founded 2018), Switch Energy (2018), eSusFarm (about 2019), Coachbit (2019) and WaFunda (2016 or 2019, depending on the source). Others are new products from older parent companies: Chronodesk (Rysis Software, about 2010), Paystashio (Byte Orbit, about 20 years) and Crystal AI (Job Crystal, about 2015). Young Aspiring Thinkers (around since at least 2021) and Intengo Market (launched around 2021 to 2022) sit right on the line. The organiser may count from a product launch or a newer company registration, which would explain it, but that isn't confirmed.
