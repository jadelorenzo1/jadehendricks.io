# SA Startup Awards 2026 Finalists: Digital Commerce and Edtech

Context: The awards are run by Startup Club ZA. Entrants must be tech ventures under five years old with a majority presence in South Africa. Finalists were announced on 10 Nov 2026, public voting ran 2 to 6 Nov, and the awards night is 18 Nov 2026. Judges score the category winners and public votes decide only the People's Choice award. — [Startup Club ZA awards page (search snippet)](https://startupclubza.com/awards/); [Disrupt Africa](https://disruptafrica.com/2026/10/26/entries-open-for-2026-south-african-startup-awards/); [Bizcommunity](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/entries-open-for-2026-south-african-startup-awards-747882a)

Method note: The awards page is rendered by JavaScript and WebFetch could not read the finalist list, so it could not be used to confirm each finalist's identity. Each match below rests on its own sources, and a confidence level is given for each one.

## What does each product do, and for whom?

### Takeaway
Digital Commerce: Berth is marina booking and management software. SERVA does in-app ordering at live events. SVYP is a gamified email-capture pop-up for e-commerce brands. Thrif is an AI-assisted marketplace for second-hand clothing. Zumii delivers school-supply kits ordered over WhatsApp.
Edtech: Bag Learning is an AI study app for university students. Coachbit is human coaching delivered through an app, focused on executive-function skills for ages 11 to 21. Edufearn makes CAPS-aligned mother-tongue resources, AR storybooks and edu-games. Young Aspiring Thinkers supports learners from disadvantaged schools. Fluent could not be identified.

### Cited Findings

**DIGITAL COMMERCE**

**1. Berth** (confidence: medium-high)
- What it is: a digital platform that lets marinas manage bookings, assign berths and communicate with boaters in one application. It won the Maritime Services & Infrastructure category at BlueInvest Africa 2026 (EU event, Nov 2026), and Matthew Wilson represented Berth at the event. — [EU Oceans & Fisheries, BlueInvest Africa 2026](https://oceans-and-fisheries.ec.europa.eu/news/blueinvest-africa-2026-where-innovation-meets-opportunities-2026-11-05_en)
- Website: berthapp.com. The site says Berth was built with South African marinas and lets boaters discover marinas and book berths, with marina operations run in the same connected system. Marinas listed include Durban Marina, Hout Bay Harbour Marina and Simon's Town Marina. — [berthapp.com](https://www.berthapp.com/); [Berth all-marinas](https://www.berthapp.com/all-marinas)
- Customers: marina operators (B2B SaaS) and boat owners looking for berths (B2C booking).
- Not found: founding year, city, full founder list (Matthew Wilson appears only as the event representative), funding.

**2. SERVA** (confidence: high)
- What it is: a mobile platform for ordering food, drinks and other items at concerts, festivals, sports events and social gatherings. Attendees order in the app, get real-time updates, and collect their items or have them delivered. Vendors get more transactions and less manual ordering work. Organisers get real-time data and reporting. — [HYPE Magazine, Mar 2026](https://hypemagazine.co.za/2026/03/18/jr-bogopa-leads-the-launch-of-serva-a-category-defining-platform-built-for-the-future-of-events/)
- Team: JR Bogopa (CEO), Emanuel Mashele (CTO), Nehemiah Sikhosana (CPO), Moihlobudi Moabelo (Chief Architect). — [HYPE Magazine](https://hypemagazine.co.za/2026/03/18/jr-bogopa-leads-the-launch-of-serva-a-category-defining-platform-built-for-the-future-of-events/)
- Traction: launched in December 2025. Its first four live-event deployments generated R300,000 in GMV, which rose to more than R500,000 by mid-February 2026 (self-reported in a launch article). — [HYPE Magazine](https://hypemagazine.co.za/2026/03/18/jr-bogopa-leads-the-launch-of-serva-a-category-defining-platform-built-for-the-future-of-events/); also covered by [Frontpage](https://frontpage.co.za/?p=22004) and [Texx and the City](https://texxandthecity.com/2026/03/serva-launches-as-new-tech-platform-aiming-to-reshape-live-event-experiences-in-south-africa/)
- Not found: website URL, city, funding.

**3. SVYP ("Swipe Your Product")** (confidence: high)
- What it is: a replacement for the email pop-up on e-commerce sites. Shoppers swipe through short image-based questions to earn the usual sign-up reward, and each answer syncs to the brand's CRM as a preference tag. It works with Shopify. — [svyp.io](https://www.svyp.io/); [my.svyp.io](https://my.svyp.io/)
- Customers: online retail and D2C brands, especially Shopify stores.
- Base: Cape Town, South Africa, according to its website. Co-founders: Nick Hoare and Richard Braithwaite. — [svyp.io](https://www.svyp.io/); [Richard Braithwaite LinkedIn](https://za.linkedin.com/in/richard-braithwaite-936001173)
- Traction (self-reported on LinkedIn): more than 150,000 shoppers have seen the swipe pop-up, and client conversion rates rose 19.4%. — [LinkedIn (James Redman reshare of Nick Hoare post)](https://www.linkedin.com/in/james-redman-2b63b4206/)
- Founding year: Braithwaite's LinkedIn post says he and Hoare launched SVYP "a year before"; the exact year was not confirmed, roughly 2024 to 2025.

**4. Thrif** (confidence: high)
- What it is: a Cape Town peer-to-peer circular fashion marketplace for new, pre-loved and vintage clothing, aimed at South African shoppers. AI tools speed up listing and improve product descriptions and images. A Thrif Wallet lets users reinvest their earnings, and sign-in and notifications run over WhatsApp. — [Crunchbase](https://crunchbase.com/organization/thrif); [TechRound](https://techround.co.uk/startups/top-7-alternatives-to-vinted/)
- Founded in 2024 by Nick Rout and Micaela De Gouveia. A newsletter describes quality-checked second-hand clothing at 50 to 70% off and says the founders are part of The Open Collab founder community. — [Crunchbase](https://crunchbase.com/organization/thrif); [The Open Letter newsletter](https://theopenletter.beehiiv.com/p/fashion-tech-sa)
- Caution: do not confuse Thrif with Drip Markets, a separate Stellenbosch student-founded thrift marketplace (Wihan Joubert, Bernard Combrink). The ITweb story on a varsity thrifting app may be about Drip. — [ITWeb](https://itweb.co.za/article/thrifting-app-made-by-varsity-students-sees-growth/wbrpOqg2bmDMDLZn)
- Not found: website URL, funding, user numbers.

**5. Zumii** (confidence: medium-high)
- What it is: a WhatsApp-based service where parents send their school-supply lists. Zumii sources the items, sorts and labels them, and delivers organised kits to the home. It also consolidates stationery and cleaning supplies for companies and schools, and runs a sponsorship programme that funds kits for students in need, with reporting for donors. — [WeAreTech Africa](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/tech-stars/south-african-entrepreneur-moloko-manthata-simplifies-school-supply-shopping-with-zumii)
- Founded in 2024 by Moloko Manthata (CEO), whose background is in hospitality and culinary work, education, and cosmetics sales and customer retention. — [WeAreTech Africa](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/tech-stars/south-african-entrepreneur-moloko-manthata-simplifies-school-supply-shopping-with-zumii)
- Caution: Zumii is not the same as Zumi, a separate B2B e-commerce company that shut down in 2023. — [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2023/03/20/zumi-shuts-down-again-letting-go-of-150-employees/)
- Not found: website, city, traction figures.

**EDTECH**

**6. Bag Learning (Pty) Ltd** (confidence: high)
- What it is: an AI study app for South African university students. Students upload their own course material, and Bag turns it into notes, flashcards, quizzes, mock exams and cheat sheets. An AI tutor called "Zazi" answers questions using only the student's material, in the app and on WhatsApp. Other features are a knowledge tree and a calendar and task manager. — [baglearning.com](https://www.baglearning.com/)
- Customers: students who buy directly, plus bursary providers and student residences that buy it for the students they support. — [baglearning.com](https://www.baglearning.com/)
- Founders: Brett Geyser, with James Pelser named as co-founder. Location is unclear: Crunchbase lists Pietermaritzburg and the Stellenbosch Network lists Stellenbosch. — [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/bag-learning); [Stellenbosch Network](https://www.stellenboschnetwork.co.za/members/bag-learning/); [Brett Geyser LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/brett-geyser-15373432b/)
- Caution: this is not BAG Innovation, a Rwandan edtech company. — [VC4A](https://vc4a.com/ventures/bag-innovation/)
- Not found: founding year, funding.

**7. Coachbit** (confidence: medium; the company is real and SA-founded, but eligibility is uncertain)
- What it is: an app that pairs each student aged 11 to 21 with a human coach. The coach provides daily accountability and builds executive-function skills (study skills, routines, life skills), rather than tutoring subjects. Customers are families and parents. — [Coachbit company profile, Welcome to the Jungle](https://app.welcometothejungle.com/companies/Coachbit); [Tutors & Exams SA](https://www.tutorsandexams.co.za/our-learning-partners/coachbit/); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.coachbit.mobile)
- Founder and CEO: Nick Miller, a UCT mechatronics engineer who founded Teach Me 2, a large one-on-one tutoring company in South Africa. — [TheOrg](https://theorg.com/org/coachbit/org-chart/nick-miller); [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/nickmillerza/)
- Founded in 2019. It claims to serve more than 10,000 families worldwide (a promotional, self-reported figure). It has 346 Trustpilot reviews averaging 4.5 out of 5. — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/coachbit); [Tutors & Exams](https://www.tutorsandexams.co.za/our-learning-partners/coachbit/); [Trustpilot](https://dk.trustpilot.com/review/coachbit.com)
- Location conflict: CB Insights lists its headquarters as Wilmington, Delaware, while the Google Play listing gives a Cape Town address. A Delaware holding company with a Cape Town operation is plausible but unconfirmed. — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/coachbit)
- Website: coachbit.com.

### Inferences
- Berth, SERVA, SVYP, Thrif, Zumii and Bag Learning are clearly South African, early-stage, recently founded ventures that fit the awards criteria.
- Coachbit was founded in 2019, so it is close to the 5-year age limit, and it has a US headquarters listing. It may qualify through a later SA entity or a "majority presence in SA" argument. This is worth noting.
- Young Aspiring Thinkers dates from 2021 or earlier, and Edufearn's commercial entity has no confirmed founding date.

## What pain point does each address (SA context)?

### Takeaway
Each startup targets a concrete local friction: manual marina admin, queues at events, weak first-party customer data, affordable sustainable fashion, the school-list scramble, a lack of personalised study support, poor study habits and executive function, a shortage of mother-tongue learning materials, and educational disadvantage in under-resourced schools.

### Cited Findings
- SERVA: "the final point of transaction" at SA live events, a sector worth billions, is inefficient, fragmented and disconnected from real-time data, which costs vendors revenue and frustrates attendees. — [HYPE Magazine](https://hypemagazine.co.za/2026/03/18/jr-bogopa-leads-the-launch-of-serva-a-category-defining-platform-built-for-the-future-of-events/)
- Berth: marinas manage bookings, berth allocation and boater communication across separate tools, and Berth combines these in one app. — [EU BlueInvest](https://oceans-and-fisheries.ec.europa.eu/news/blueinvest-africa-2026-where-innovation-meets-opportunities-2026-11-05_en)
- SVYP: standard email pop-ups collect an address but no preference data, while SVYP's swipe questions record what each shopper likes in the CRM. — [svyp.io](https://www.svyp.io/)
- Thrif: offers affordable fashion (50 to 70% off) and circularity. Its WhatsApp-native design suits SA users, citing 96% WhatsApp reach among SA internet users. — [Crunchbase](https://crunchbase.com/organization/thrif); [The Open Letter](https://theopenletter.beehiiv.com/p/fashion-tech-sa)
- Zumii: simplifies the time-consuming job of buying school supplies for families, and gives donors a traceable way to sponsor kits for learners in need. — [WeAreTech Africa](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/tech-stars/south-african-entrepreneur-moloko-manthata-simplifies-school-supply-shopping-with-zumii)
- Bag Learning: gives university students personalised study aids and tutoring at scale, and bursary funders can buy it for their students. — [baglearning.com](https://www.baglearning.com/)
- Coachbit: the founder's 20 years in tutoring showed him that capable students often struggle because of executive-function and accountability gaps rather than subject knowledge. — [Welcome to the Jungle](https://app.welcometothejungle.com/companies/Coachbit)
- Edufearn: tackles the learning gap in under-resourced SA schools with bilingual and mother-tongue CAPS materials. — [NASCEE](https://nascee.org.za/membership/members/the-edufearn-foundation)
- Young Aspiring Thinkers: supports learners from disadvantaged schools. — [Podcast](https://audible.com.au/podcast/Episode-46-Thulani-Masebenza-Young-Aspiring-Thinkers/B09KH69LY1)

### Inferences
- Bag Learning, Zumii and Thrif all use WhatsApp as a core channel, which reflects how South African consumers use mobile.

- Fluent's problem statement is unknown because the company was not identified.
